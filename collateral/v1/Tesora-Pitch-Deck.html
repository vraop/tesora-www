The Problem

Where the month goes

Most of the actuarial month is spent getting data ready.

A mid-sized specialty team runs roughly 150 hours a month of recurring actuarial work. The majority of it goes to reconciling data and reformatting exhibits. The selections that actually move a loss ratio come last.

Every hour spent on intake is an hour taken from the market, the mix, and the next filing.

The single biggest block is data extraction and reconciliation, the least actuarial part of an actuary's month.

What It Costs

Three costs follow from that, and every specialty carrier feels all three.

Rate filing

Filings lag the market

Each state filing is weeks of assembling exhibits and reformatting them to the next template. By the time a rate is live, the trend it was built on has moved.

Rate adequacy erodes before it goes live.
Competitive read

The market is a blind spot

Competitor SERFF filings rarely get read, because reading them by hand takes days. So carriers price against their own history instead of the market they compete in.

You price on your own book alone.
Capacity

The team is the ceiling

Every new line, state, or study competes for the same few credentialed actuaries. Growth means hiring, and credentialed hires are slow and scarce.

Growth is capped at your hiring rate.

The Harness

The Workbench is the surface. The Tesora Harness is the framework beneath it that turns a fast answer into one you can put your name on. Four agents do the work, and not one of them gets the last word.

Ingestion AgentReads

Takes loss runs, SOVs, dec pages, and rate manuals in whatever format they arrive, and normalizes them underneath the workflow you already run.

Insight AgentModels

Fits the curves, trends, and on-levels, then hands back a write-up the reviewer can check line by line.

Rating AgentDeploys

Turns the actuary's selections into a tested rater and the state exhibits that go with it, ready to file.

Audit AgentProves

Traces every value to a page, a row, a cell, and writes down anything it skipped, so a capped limit lands in the memo where a reviewer will see it.

Author, reviewer, and approver stay three separate people, enforced. Every value cites its source, the log is append-only, and the actuary owns every decision.
